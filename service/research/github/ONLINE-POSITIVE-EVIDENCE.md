# Online positive contact-action evidence, wave 1

21 October 2026. Model-blind, CPU-only source acquisition and provisional assistant review.

**6 eligible contact-proxy recordings from 3 conservative source groups**, 39.000 seconds of annotated positive episode spans, and 2.400 seconds of explicitly inspected ordinary-motion lead-out. These are sports/practice contact proxies, not verified hostile fights. The lower end of the 6-10-recording target is met. Two downloaded exclusions are retained separately and do not pad the positive count. Eligible recordings total 146.425 declared source seconds; only exported reviewed event intervals may be scored, not that whole duration.

The frozen review bundle is [wave1-positive-review.json](../evaluation/wave1-positive-review.json). Paths in that bundle resolve relative to its own directory. Raw media, source-rights pins, PTS arrays, timestamped images, local utilities and [validation receipt](../cache/eval-wave1/positives/validation.json) are in the assigned ignored [positives cache](../cache/eval-wave1/positives/). No shared scripts, corpus, inventories, completed quality files, model scores or live services were modified or executed.

## Review evidence and clocks

OpenCV full-source passes used one decoder sequentially and `cv2.setNumThreads(1)`; all eight sources together contain **7,228 decoded frames**, with strictly increasing actual `CAP_PROP_POS_MSEC`. Each source was visually screened using two timestamped sheets containing 32 approximately evenly spaced actual decoded frames. Each retained positive additionally has 32 denser timestamp-driven samples in a selected interval. Tokyo was enlarged with an evidence-only crop at original source coordinates `[300,460,1550,1000]`; the working video remains uncropped. All saved contact sheets and Tokyo detail frames were opened with `view_image`. Execution deviation: the dense and all-frame CPU evidence utilities briefly overlapped on the last two sources after the dense command yielded a running session; each utility had one decoder/threads=1. This did not meet the requested global one-decoder bound during that brief overlap. Both completed and all evidence clocks/hashes were checked; no model/GPU job ran.

Every chronological decoded frame in selected central contact subspans was additionally inspected: **621 frames across 24.284 seconds**. Exact boundaries, frame counts and metadata paths are in `allFramesReviewedContactIntervals`; these directly support the exported contact episodes. Hon-kesa-gatame's all-frame inspection extends slightly outside its more conservative exported event; those extra margins remain unscored. Buganda-ekigwo's subset is 3.960 seconds rather than rounded up to four. Every one of 58 frames in the 2.400-second ordinary-motion lead-out was also inspected. This was chronological still-frame inspection, not real-time video playback. Wider episode boundaries remain conservative provisional interpolation; unannotated margins/tails remain `unknownIntervals` and are never negative time. The ordinary subset is shorter than a four-second training window and must not be stretched.

Independent sequential FFmpeg `showinfo` decoding checked all 7,228 paired frame timestamps and frame counts. The binary SHA256 was verified before use: `2ce797a0f88d7f067180338fb227f7b1928ea727bd9a4d7a1d022f7c52af71a3` (pinned Gyan FFmpeg 7.1, CPU threads 2, no hardware acceleration). Source integer PTS/time base, full command arguments and logs are retained per source. For Tokyo, all Buganda files and both Hvja files, `source PTS ms = OpenCV PTS ms + 3`; for both Ekpeye files, offset is zero. The maximum all-frame residual is below 0.000000001 ms (well below 1 ms). Exported event coordinates use **FFmpeg source milliseconds**, not silently rebased OpenCV time. Parent adapters must apply the explicit offset when using an OpenCV clock. No nominal FPS was used to manufacture timestamps. Declared durations and last observed frame times are retained separately; short final unobserved margins remain unknown.

All eligible groups happen to be reserved audit; **zero development groups** were produced. Names/roles were not changed to balance them. These labels must stay model-blind until the parent's selected frozen audit comparison. Different source groups do not establish actor independence or absence of overlap with publisher/pretraining data. No existing boxing/grappling/kick/squats source URL or media SHA256 was reused, and no VID footage was introduced.

Downloaded exclusions are explicit, with no events: `ekpeye-wrestling` showed ceremony footage and black frames rather than a contact match in the actual samples; `eta-wrestling` has visible grappling but prominent adjacent staffs/sticks, foreground occlusion/cuts and a black tail, so it was conservatively excluded from this strict unarmed/no-prop pool. Neither is silently relabelled normal. The potentially useful Eta contact is retained for traceability, not a hidden positive omission based on model behaviour.

[Hvja's Kata-juji-jime](https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?oldid=1251945448), 17.763s / 17,331,443 bytes, and [Hon-kesa-gatame](https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?oldid=1184344219), 25.805s / 25,148,855 bytes, were both downloaded and independently reviewed after the requested cooldown. Kata initially received HTTP 429 with `Retry-After: 600`; the failed receipt is preserved and the subsequent bounded retry succeeded. No title/licence-only positive approval was used. Actual frames show controlled partner ground holds; these modest-motion demonstrations are contact proxies, not vigorous hostile fights or technique-efficacy evidence.

Other researched leads were not used: Local Wrestling is 168 seconds and would need an explicitly tracked bounded working excerpt; a large judo tournament exceeds the original download cap; Professional wrestling top-rope training does not establish person-contact from the description; mirrored YouTube-derived Grappling Session lacked primary author-release verification; Kunkhmerclinchfighting had an unresolved deletion/rights concern. The suggested Rumble in the Deid lead was researched but not acquired: its asset-specific DVIDS primary pages were inaccessible to the web tool, and a general government association/public-domain FAQ is insufficient to certify an individual asset. The [DVIDS copyright notice](https://www.dvidshub.net/about/copyright) itself distinguishes official government works from third-party rights and retains privacy/endorsement limits. No forms, accounts, external messages or agreements were used.

## Checks and remaining gaps

The local validation utility checked JSON parsing, unique IDs, owned-cache path containment, actual media SHA256/publisher SHA1/size, exclusion of existing corpus hashes/URLs, deterministic roles, every paired source/OpenCV frame clock, monotonic PTS and <=2000 frames/source, events within observed source content, positive spans >=4 seconds, exact event/unknown duration partitions without overlap or gaps, excluded records with no events, and evidence SHA256. All **8 downloaded records passed**. Actual downloaded media: **150,502,794 bytes**, below 300,000,000. No installs or model/GPU runs. The original four-source evaluation corpus was read only for URL/hash exclusion.

Missing: licensed clear contact sparring/takedown diversity beyond the retained grappling examples, enough independent groups, any development role in this collection, verified native camera raster, measured person pixel heights/counts, true near-IR, thermal IR, low-light colour, representative compressed CCTV, and a validated 50-100-person operating condition. Colour daylight/indoor sport footage and distant practice pairs do not fill those gaps. No accuracy, transfer, operational crowd/night capability, participant identity, intent or injury claim is supported.
