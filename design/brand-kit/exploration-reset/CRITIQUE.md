# Rough-symbol critique

This is the assistant's design judgement, not measured user preference, a recognition test or trademark assessment. Apply the professional-logo-design rubric at rough fidelity: silhouette, brief fit, simplicity, coherence and meaning can be discussed; actual vector small-size versatility is still untested. Name legibility is inapplicable because the user prohibits letter-based invention and these sheets omit the name.

No first-pass rough is accepted as a final logo. Advance only the protected-view relationship into a controlled eight-variant study. It must shed headgear/camera resemblance and work without a pupil, border or wordmark trick. This is an internal next experiment, not a user-selected direction.

## Controlled study / D1–D8

Result: do not advance this family as drawn. Simplification removed the view/cover relationship and left cap-like or bent-stroke silhouettes. D1/D2/D4/D6/D8 are too similar in topology; D3 suggests a sports visor/swoosh; D5 suggests a generic helmet or shelter; D7 suggests a cap or bracket. This is useful negative evidence: reducing the overhang alone does not create a convincing identity.

## Outcome of this exploration

32 visual roughs inspected; no final identity approved or production candidate claimed. Two ideas remain useful for a later experiment, not as exact marks: A3's figure/ground construction and B1/B6's combination of shelter and sight. The mask, helmet and cap silhouettes themselves should not be carried forward. C's arbitrary interception geometry and the round-11 literal animals/beacons should not be rescued by adding colour or mockups.

This phase does not ask the user to choose among failed roughs. A future presentation of three refined concepts must first pass stronger internal critique. Colour, typography, app-icon exports and complete kits remain downstream of a convincing flat-symbol direction.
